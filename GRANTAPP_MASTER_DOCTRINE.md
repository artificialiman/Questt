## III. THE BATTLE DOCTRINE — Strategic Subject Framing

Before any question is written, any fact-file assembled, or any session run —
every agent and collaborator must understand the **strategic role of each subject** on the battlefield.

This is not metaphor for motivation. This is operational intelligence.

---

### ENGLISH — The Open Secret Weapon

Everybody sees it. Nobody respects it enough.

English is not infantry. It is not artillery. It is the **intelligence layer** — present in every other subject's questions, quietly determining who wins and who loses before the subject knowledge even matters.

JAMB does not just test English in the English section. It deploys English as an obfuscation instrument across Chemistry, Physics, Mathematics, Biology — every subject. The hardest Chemistry question is often not hard because of Chemistry. It is hard because of how it is written.

The scholar who masters English is not just passing English. They are **decoding the entire battlefield.**

Lethal precisely because it is invisible. The open weapon that is actually secret.

**English obfuscation toolkit JAMB deploys across all subjects:**
- Double negatives in question stems ("Which is NOT an incorrect statement...")
- Passive voice hiding the agent ("The electrode at which oxidation occurs...")
- Conditional phrasing burying the qualifier ("...would be least likely to...")
- Nominalization turning processes into objects ("The decomposition of..." vs "When it decomposes...")
- Embedded clauses that bury the subject before the verb arrives

**English preparation has two tracks — both are non-negotiable:**
1. **English as a subject** — Oral, Lexis, Comprehension, Concord, Idioms. Pass the 40 questions.
2. **English as a decoder** — Train scholars to surgically strip any question in any subject to its bare logical skeleton before answering. Strip the negatives. Find the true subject. Locate the actual question buried in the sentence. Then answer with subject knowledge.

### MATHEMATICS — The Big Bad Cannon

Loud. Obvious. Everyone knows it is dangerous.

Mathematics is raw power. When it fires cleanly, nothing survives. But its deeper function is as a **force multiplier** — every subject it touches becomes heavier, more precise, more devastating.

Physics calls it and becomes unstoppable (projectiles, mirrors, waves, optics).
Chemistry borrows it and becomes exact (Faraday, mole calculations, gas laws, pH).
Biology uses it quietly (genetics ratios, population ecology, osmotic pressure).

The scholar who owns Mathematics does not just pass Mathematics.
They **upgrade every other subject simultaneously.**

The cannon is most dangerous when it fires through another subject's territory.

### CHEMISTRY — The Complex High-Tech Hybrid

Submarine on the surface. Destroyer underneath.

Chemistry operates on **multiple levels simultaneously** — physical, mathematical, conceptual, nomenclatural, industrial. It is the subject where the same word means two different things in the same equation, where the notation is dense, the analogies are scarce, and the exceptions are surgical.

Electrolysis looks like electricity until it becomes moles.
Equilibrium looks like balance until it is Kc, Kp, temperature, pressure, and catalyst shifting simultaneously.
Pressure wears so many costumes across so many topics it constitutes its own syndicate.

Most scholars think they understand Chemistry until the question goes underwater.

The scholar who masters Chemistry has learned to operate in **multiple registers at once.**
That is a cognitive skill, not merely a subject skill.

### PHYSICS — The One That Always Simply Works

Deceptively clean on the surface. Everyone prepares for it because everyone can see its shape.

But Physics' real power is **systemic integration** — it absorbs everything around it and still functions. Its principles are so fundamental they underpin everything else.

- Calls Mathematics → becomes the big bad cannon with precision targeting (projectiles, mirrors, waves)
- Borrows Chemistry's territory → oversimplifies it into clean working principles (heat, electrolysis, pressure as a scalar)
- Stands alone → still works, still scores, still reliable

Physics is the conventional system that defeats unconventional problems because its laws are universal.
The scholar who masters Physics has the most **transferable mental model** on the battlefield.

## IV. THE TRIAGE DOCTRINE — Sprint, Return, Pro Ceiling

Negative marking changes everything. The question is never only "do you know it?"

The three-tier triage every scholar must internalize before sitting any paper:

**SPRINT** — I recognize this. Low friction. Known territory. Answer and move.
Time budget: under 60 seconds. No hesitation, no second-guessing.

**RETURN** — I know this territory but it needs time and full focus.
Flag it. Move on. Come back with time banked from sprints.
Time budget: 2–4 minutes when returned to.

**PRO CEILING** — This is a depth marker. The 5 questions JAMB doesn't expect tutors to teach.
Attempt only if: (a) time allows, and (b) you can eliminate at least 2 distractors confidently.
Never bleed marks on a blind gamble. A controlled 50/50 after elimination is acceptable.
A pure 4-option guess is not.

**The Messi principle applied to triage:**
He did not dribble every defender. He knew in under a second — take him on, lay it off, or hold.
That decision speed is the system. Triage is not hesitation. Triage is trained recognition.

**The triage skill is trained, not natural.**
Every question in the GrantEd bank carries a triage tag. Scholars learn the tags before they learn to answer.

## V. THE FRICTION ATLAS — Where JAMB Actually Bites

The friction atlas is GrantEd's most classified asset.

It is not a topic list. It is a **terrain map** — every place in every subject where notation is non-intuitive, where the same word means two different things, where analogies break down, where the hidden variable quietly changes the answer.

The syllabus lists topics. The friction atlas reveals **why specific topics produce wrong answers in prepared students.**

### THE QUIET EXCEPTIONAL FACT

The most important taxonomy in the atlas.

A quiet exceptional fact is a piece of knowledge that:
- Is rarely taught explicitly because most tutors don't rate it as teachable
- Is considered by the examining board as a marker of genuine subject depth
- Lives at the intersection of notation, exception, or edge case
- Appears in the pro ceiling tier — the 5 questions that separate coached from educated

The quiet exceptional fact is not trivia. It is the point where the subject reveals its actual structure to those who looked deeply enough.

### MATHEMATICS FRICTION ATLAS

#### 1. Binary Operations
**Friction type:** Symbol means nothing until defined per question.
The operation is invented each time. Every question is a new language.

**Where it bites:**
- Commutativity assumed without proof — `a★b ≠ b★a` unless demonstrated
- Associativity tested across two separate substitutions — students collapse into one
- Identity element: must work both sides — `a★e = a` AND `e★a = a`
- Inverse depends on identity — one wrong, both wrong
- Nested operations evaluated outside-in instead of inside-out

**Quiet exceptional fact:** The identity element is not always 0 or 1. It is whatever value satisfies the defined operation. Students import arithmetic intuition into an invented system.

**Triage tag:** Return. Notation recognition is fast once trained; execution needs care.

---

#### 2. Modular Arithmetic
**Friction type:** The ≡ symbol looks like equals. It is not equals.

`17 ≡ 2 (mod 5)` means same remainder on division by 5. Not equality.

**Where it bites:**
- Negative numbers: `-3 (mod 5) = 2` not `-3`. Students leave it negative.
- Clock arithmetic: mod 12 means 13 ≡ 1, not 0.
- Solving congruences requires modular inverse, not regular division.
- Students solve normally then forget to reduce `(mod n)` at the final step.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** Modular inverse exists only when gcd(a, n) = 1. JAMB occasionally tests this boundary condition.

**Triage tag:** Return. The ≡ symbol is the only wall. Once decoded, execution is mechanical.

---

#### 3. Logic (Symbolic Logic)
**Friction type:** Entire vocabulary is new with no everyday equivalent. Symbols combine.

| Symbol | Meaning | Core friction |
|---|---|---|
| `~p` | NOT p | Deadly inside compound statements |
| `p ∧ q` | p AND q | Both must be true |
| `p ∨ q` | p OR q | Inclusive or — students assume exclusive |
| `p → q` | If p then q | The contrapositive trap |
| `p ↔ q` | p iff q | Both directions must hold |

**The contrapositive trap — the single most exploited friction point in logic:**
`p → q` is equivalent to `~q → ~p` (contrapositive — VALID)
NOT equivalent to `~p → ~q` (inverse — INVALID)
NOT equivalent to `q → p` (converse — INVALID)

**Truth table trap:** `p → q` is false ONLY when p is true and q is false. Students mark it false whenever q is false regardless of p.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** The biconditional `p ↔ q` is true when both have the same truth value — true-true OR false-false. Students only remember the true-true case.

**Triage tag:** Sprint for single symbol. Return for contrapositive/truth table. Pro ceiling for nested compound 3-variable statements.

---

#### 4. Set Theory
**Friction type:** Three languages — symbolic, Venn diagram, word problem — deployed interchangeably.

**The formula trap:**
`n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A∩B)` — taught everywhere.

Then JAMB adds a third set:
`n(A∪B∪C) = n(A) + n(B) + n(C) - n(A∩B) - n(A∩C) - n(B∩C) + n(A∩B∩C)`

The `+n(A∩B∩C)` at the end is always dropped.

**The word problem costume:** "Neither" means subtract from total BEFORE applying the formula. Students apply formula to full total.

**The complement trap:** `n(A') = n(U) - n(A)`. The Universal set shifts with context. Students use the wrong total as U.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** Set difference is directional. `A\B ≠ B\A`. Students treat it as commutative.

**Triage tag:** Sprint if Venn values are given directly. Return for 2-set word problem. Pro ceiling for 3-set with complement and partial information.

---

#### 5. Partial Fractions
**Friction type:** Decomposition form must be chosen correctly before any calculation. Wrong form — everything fails.

**The four costumes most tutors collapse into one:**

| Denominator type | Correct form | What students write |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct linear: `(x+1)(x+2)` | `A/(x+1) + B/(x+2)` | Correct — only case taught |
| Repeated linear: `(x+1)(x+2)²` | `A/(x+1) + B/(x+2) + C/(x+2)²` | Missing the `C/(x+2)²` term |
| Irreducible quadratic: `(x+1)(x²+4)` | `A/(x+1) + (Bx+C)/(x²+4)` | Writing `B/(x²+4)` — numerator never balances |
| Improper fraction (degree num ≥ denom) | Long division first, then decompose | Decomposing the improper fraction directly |

**The reverse integration principle:** Partial fractions exist to make complex fractions integrable. Each decomposed term integrates to a clean `ln` expression. Teaching this "why" makes the improper fraction step obvious — of course you divide first, you cannot integrate an improper fraction directly.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** The cover-up method works only for distinct linear factors. It partially works for repeated linear. It fails completely for quadratic factors. Students who only know cover-up are defenseless.

**Triage tag:** Return always. Pro ceiling if improper + quadratic combined.

---

#### 6. Binomial Theorem
**Friction type:** Standard expansion is taught. The exceptions are the pro ceiling.

**Standard:** `(a+b)ⁿ = Σ C(n,r) aⁿ⁻ʳ bʳ` — handled by most prepared students.

**The exceptions:**

| Exception | The trap |
|---|---|
| Specific term | T(r+1) = C(n,r)... — term 3 means r=2 not r=3. One index off, confident wrong answer. |
| Independent term | Set power of x to zero, solve for r. Students don't know this question type exists. |
| Negative/fractional index | Infinite series valid only for `\|x\| < 1`. Students apply finite binomial formula. |
| Approximation | `(0.98)^6` — students don't recognise this as a binomial question. |

**Quiet exceptional fact:** For `(1+x)^(1/2)`, the expansion is valid only when `|x| < 1`. The validity condition is frequently the actual question, not the expansion itself.

**Triage tag:** Sprint for straightforward expansion. Return for specific term. Pro ceiling for fractional index, independent term, approximation.

---

#### 7. Circle Theorem
**Friction type:** Standard theorems are taught. The exceptions have no intuitive derivation.

**Standard (taught everywhere):** Angle at centre, angles in same segment, angle in semicircle.

**The exceptions:**

| Exception | Why it bites |
|---|---|
| Alternate segment theorem | Angle between tangent and chord equals inscribed angle on opposite side. The tangent is outside the circle. No intuition available. |
| Cyclic quadrilateral algebra | Opposite angles sum to 180°. JAMB gives 3 algebraic angles, asks for exterior angle. Three hidden steps. |
| Tangent-tangent from external point | Two tangents from same point are equal. Students reach for trigonometry instead. |
| Intersecting chords: `PA×PB = PC×PD` | No obvious derivation from standard theorems. Looks arbitrary without deep familiarity. |

**Triage tag:** Sprint for standard angle theorem. Return for cyclic quadrilateral algebra. Pro ceiling for alternate segment + intersecting chords combined.

---

#### 8. Remainder & Factor Theorem
**Friction type:** The divisor form changes the substitution value.

**Standard:** `f(a) = 0` means `(x-a)` is a factor. Remainder of `f(x) ÷ (x-a)` is `f(a)`.

**Where it bites:**

| Exception | The trap |
|---|---|
| Divisor `(ax-b)` | Substitute `x = b/a` not `x = a`. Students see the coefficient and substitute it directly. |
| Two unknowns | Two remainders generate two equations — simultaneous system in disguise. Students solve one equation with two unknowns and stop. |
| Remainder as expression | Dividing by quadratic `(x-1)(x-2)` leaves linear remainder `3x-1` not a constant. Students expect a number, get an expression, freeze. |

**Triage tag:** Sprint for straightforward factor/root. Return for `(ax-b)` divisor. Pro ceiling for remainder as expression or simultaneous setup.

---

#### 9. AP & GP — The Quiet Exceptional Fact Series
**Friction type:** Appears elementary. Exceptions are surgical.

| Exception | The trap |
|---|---|
| Sum to infinity | Valid only when `\|r\| < 1`. JAMB gives r > 1. Correct answer: undefined. Students calculate anyway. |
| Mixed AP/GP | Three numbers in AP. Same numbers modified in GP. Two separate condition equations needed simultaneously. |
| Inserting means | Insert 4 geometric means between 2 and 486 → 6-term sequence, not 5. Wrong n from line one. |

**Triage tag:** Sprint for nth term and standard sum. Return for sum to infinity with r-check. Pro ceiling for mixed sequences and inserted means.

### CHEMISTRY FRICTION ATLAS

#### THE PVT SYNDICATE

Pressure, Volume, and Temperature are Chemistry's most cross-contaminating variables. Pressure alone wears more costumes across more topics than any other quantity in the UTME syllabus. It is the most-questioned variable in the science stream.

**Pressure's costumes:**
- Gas laws: Boyle's Law, Charles' Law, combined gas law
- Equilibrium: Kp expressions, partial pressures
- Electrode reactions: volume of gas evolved at STP
- Colligative properties: osmotic pressure
- Henry's Law: gas solubility under pressure

Same symbol P. Completely different formula. Different context. Different units. Each one a potential trap.

---

#### 1. Electrolysis — The Syndicate Boss
**Friction type:** Multiple n-values in one calculation chain. Same word, three different quantities.

**The calculation chain every scholar must own:**
```
Current (A) × Time (s) → Charge Q (coulombs)
Q ÷ 96500 → Moles of electrons
Moles of electrons ÷ n → Moles of substance
Moles of substance × Molar mass → Mass deposited/dissolved
```

**Every arrow hides a variable:**
- `Q = It` — straightforward but the entry point only
- `moles of e⁻ = Q ÷ 96500` — the Faraday constant, students sometimes use 96000
- **The n problem:** n = number of electrons transferred per ion. Cu²⁺ → n=2. Al³⁺ → n=3. Na⁺ → n=1. Students forget to divide by n, or use the wrong n.
- **The two-faced mole:** "moles of electrons" ≠ "moles of substance" ≠ "moles of ions." Three quantities, one word.

**The electrode layer on top:**
- Cathode/anode behaviour flips between molten and aqueous electrolytes
- Selective discharge: concentration and position in electrochemical series both matter
- Faraday's second law: comparing two substances in series cells using molar mass ÷ n ratios

**Quiet exceptional fact:** In Faraday's second law problems, the ratio compared is `(molar mass ÷ n)` not molar mass alone. Students who compare molar masses directly get plausible wrong answers.

**Triage tag:** Return for single-variable Faraday calculation. Pro ceiling for selective discharge + multi-substance comparison in series.

---

#### 2. Chemical Equilibrium — The Chameleon
**Friction type:** One principle wearing six simultaneous costumes.

**Where it bites:**

| Problem type | The specific trap |
|---|---|
| Le Chatelier combined | Pressure + temperature + concentration changed simultaneously. Students apply one at a time, miss the net effect. |
| Kc expression | Stoichiometric powers are mandatory. `2NO₂ ⇌ N₂O₄` gives `Kc = [N₂O₄]/[NO₂]²` not `[N₂O₄]/[NO₂]`. |
| Kp vs Kc | Same equilibrium, different expression, different units. `Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn`. Students conflate them. |
| Heterogeneous equilibrium | Solids and pure liquids omitted from expression. No intuition for why. Students include them. |
| Shifting vs changing K | Catalyst, temperature changes K itself. Concentration and pressure only shift position. Students say catalyst increases K. |
| Partial pressures | Mole fraction × total pressure = partial pressure. Δn must be calculated first. Students skip mole fraction step. |

**Quiet exceptional fact:** Adding an inert gas at constant volume does not shift equilibrium. Adding it at constant pressure does (by reducing partial pressures). JAMB distinguishes between these two scenarios.

**Triage tag:** Return for single-variable Le Chatelier. Pro ceiling for Kp calculation with Δn or combined variable shift.

---

#### 3. Mole Concept & Stoichiometry
**Friction type:** The mole is a bridge concept and students treat it as an endpoint.

**The chain students must execute without error:**
```
Mass → Moles → Mole ratio → Moles of product → Mass/Volume/Concentration
```

**Where it bites:**
- Limiting reagent: students use the first reagent given, not the one that runs out first
- Moles of gas at STP: 1 mole = 22.4 L. Students use this for non-STP conditions.
- Concentration: `C = n/V`. V must be in litres. Students leave it in cm³.
- Percentage yield: `(actual ÷ theoretical) × 100`. Students invert the fraction.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** Molar volume at STP is 22.4 L (old STP: 0°C, 1 atm) vs 22.7 L (new STP: 0°C, 100 kPa). JAMB uses 22.4. Students who memorize 22.7 from newer references get wrong answers.

**Triage tag:** Sprint for direct mole-mass conversion. Return for limiting reagent. Pro ceiling for percentage yield combined with limiting reagent.

---

#### 4. Atomic Structure & Periodicity
**Quiet exceptional facts only — standard content is well-taught.**

| Quiet exceptional fact | Why it bites |
|---|---|
| Electronic configuration of exceptions | Cr is [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ not [Ar]3d⁴4s². Cu is [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ not [Ar]3d⁹4s². Half-filled and fully-filled d subshells are more stable. |
| Ionization energy dip | IE dip between group 2 and 3 (2s² → 2p¹ easier to remove). IE dip between group 5 and 6 (paired p electrons repel). Students expect monotonic increase. |
| Electronegativity vs electron affinity | Not the same. Electronegativity is bonding tendency. Electron affinity is energy change for isolated anion formation. |
| Isoelectronic species | Same number of electrons, different nuclear charge. Na⁺, Ne, F⁻, O²⁻, N³⁻ are all isoelectronic. Students confuse with isotopes. |

---

#### 5. Organic Chemistry Nomenclature
**Friction type:** IUPAC rules have a strict priority hierarchy students rarely learn completely.

**Where it bites:**
- Longest chain rule: the chain must include the principal functional group
- Numbering direction: lowest locants for substituents, but principal group takes priority
- Cyclic compounds: prefix "cyclo" before parent chain name
- Multiple functional groups: priority order (carboxylic acid > aldehyde > ketone > alcohol > amine)

**Quiet exceptional fact:** When two chains of equal length exist, choose the one with the greater number of substituents. Students always default to the first chain they find.

**Triage tag:** Return for standard IUPAC naming. Pro ceiling for multi-functional group priority conflict.

### PHYSICS FRICTION ATLAS

#### 1. Projectile Motion — Calls the Cannon
**Friction type:** One object, two simultaneous equations, time is the only shared variable.

```
Horizontal: uniform motion     → x = u cosθ × t         (a = 0)
Vertical:   accelerated motion → y = u sinθ × t - ½gt²   (a = g)
```

**Where it bites:**
- Students solve one equation without recognizing the other is needed
- Time of flight derived from vertical equation is substituted into horizontal — students don't make this connection
- `Range = u²sin2θ/g` — valid only for launch and landing at same height. JAMB varies this.
- Maximum range at 45° — quiet exceptional fact: two angles give the same range (θ and 90°-θ)
- Horizontal launch (θ=0): simplifies to `x = ut`, `y = ½gt²`. Students use the angled equations.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** Complementary angles produce equal ranges. 30° and 60° give identical horizontal range. JAMB tests this as a "which of the following also gives the same range" question.

**Triage tag:** Return for single-variable projectile. Pro ceiling for equal-range angle question or variable landing height.

---

#### 2. Mirrors & Lenses — Wickedly Deep
**Friction type:** Sign convention is the first wall. The formula looks identical for both mirrors and lenses. The behaviour is opposite.

**Mirror formula:** `1/f = 1/v + 1/u`
**Lens formula:** `1/f = 1/v - 1/u`

They look almost the same. They are not.

**Sign convention trap:** Real-is-positive convention vs New Cartesian convention — JAMB uses both in different questions. Students pick one and apply universally.

**Magnification trap:**
- m = v/u for mirrors, m = -v/u for lenses
- m > 1: magnified. m < 1: diminished. Positive m: upright. Negative m: inverted.
- All four combinations are possible. Students only know "bigger = magnified."

**Concave mirror behaviour by object position:**
| Object position | Image type |
|---|---|
| Beyond C | Real, inverted, diminished |
| At C | Real, inverted, same size |
| Between C and F | Real, inverted, magnified |
| At F | No image (at infinity) |
| Inside F | Virtual, upright, magnified |

**Quiet exceptional fact:** A convex mirror ALWAYS produces a virtual, upright, diminished image regardless of object position. No exceptions. JAMB tests whether students know this is absolute.

**Triage tag:** Return for single mirror/lens calculation. Pro ceiling for combined optical system or magnification type identification with sign convention conflict.

---

#### 3. Waves & Sound
**Quiet exceptional facts only — standard content is accessible.**

| Quiet exceptional fact | Why it bites |
|---|---|
| Stationary waves: nodes and antinodes | Node = zero displacement always. Antinode = maximum displacement. Distance between adjacent node and antinode = λ/4. Students use λ/2. |
| Resonance in pipes: open vs closed | Open pipe: both ends antinodes, harmonics are all integers (f, 2f, 3f...). Closed pipe: one end node, only odd harmonics (f, 3f, 5f...). Students apply open pipe formula to closed pipe. |
| Doppler effect direction | Approaching source: frequency increases. Receding: decreases. Students flip the formula when the observer moves instead of the source. |
| Beats frequency | `f_beat = |f₁ - f₂|`. Always positive. Always the absolute difference. Students calculate with sign and get negative beats. |

---

#### 4. Electricity & Magnetism
**Quiet exceptional facts only.**

| Quiet exceptional fact | Why it bites |
|---|---|
| Internal resistance | Terminal voltage = EMF - Ir. Under load, terminal voltage drops. Students use EMF directly as terminal voltage. |
| Wheatstone bridge balance condition | `P/Q = R/S`. Balance means NO current through galvanometer. Students think balance means maximum current. |
| Transformer efficiency | Ideal transformer: `VₚIₚ = VsIs`. Real transformer efficiency < 100%. JAMB gives efficiency percentage — students ignore it. |
| Right-hand rule orientation | Standard orientation is taught. JAMB rotates the setup. Students can't apply the rule to non-standard orientations. |

### LITERATURE-IN-ENGLISH FRICTION ATLAS

Literature operates on three attack vectors simultaneously. Understanding which vector a question targets is itself the skill.

#### The Three Attack Vectors

**PROSE — The surface narrative hides the deep structure**
Questions are rarely about what happened. They are about why it happened, how the author signals it, and what it means beyond the plot.

Friction points:
- **Narrative perspective:** First person unreliable narrator — the narrator lies or is wrong. Students trust the narrator.
- **Characterization by omission:** What a character does NOT say or do. Students only catalogue actions.
- **Thematic irony:** The stated theme and the demonstrated theme contradict. JAMB asks for demonstrated.
- **Diction questions:** A word is used — students pick the dictionary meaning instead of the contextual meaning.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** In prose, "tone" is the author's attitude to the subject. "Mood" is the reader/character's emotional response. JAMB distinguishes these precisely. Students use them interchangeably.

---

**DRAMA — The stage directions are not decoration**
Drama questions require reading the text AND the implied performance.

Friction points:
- **Dramatic irony:** Audience knows what characters don't. JAMB asks what the audience feels, not what the character feels.
- **Soliloquy vs aside vs monologue:** Students conflate. Soliloquy = alone on stage to self. Aside = to audience, unheard by characters. Monologue = long speech to other characters present.
- **Tragic flaw (hamartia):** Must be a quality that is also a strength in other contexts. Students identify only the destructive aspect.
- **Catharsis:** The audience's emotional release, not the character's. Students assign it to the protagonist.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** In tragedy, the protagonist must be responsible for their own downfall through choice, not merely fate. JAMB tests whether students understand this distinction in context.

---

**POETRY — The form is part of the meaning**
The most notation-heavy section of Literature. Every technical term is a precise tool.

Friction points:
- **Metre vs rhythm:** Metre is the pattern. Rhythm is how it actually sounds in performance. They can differ.
- **Enjambment effect:** Line runs on without pause — creates speed, urgency, breathlessness. Students identify it without saying what it does.
- **Caesura effect:** Mid-line pause — creates emphasis, hesitation, contrast. Same problem.
- **Persona vs poet:** The speaker of the poem is not the poet unless the poem is explicitly autobiographical. JAMB tests this distinction.
- **Connotation questions:** Students give denotative (dictionary) answer. Correct answer is the emotional/cultural resonance.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** Irony in poetry operates at three levels simultaneously — verbal (says one thing means another), situational (outcome contradicts expectation), and dramatic (reader knows more than speaker). JAMB often asks which type of irony is present. Students identify irony but cannot specify the type.

### BIOLOGY FRICTION ATLAS

#### 1. Genetics — The Notation Minefield
**Friction type:** Multiple notation systems for the same concept. Phenotype and genotype look identical until they don't.

**Where it bites:**

| Exception | The trap |
|---|---|
| Co-dominance vs incomplete dominance | Co-dominance: both alleles fully expressed (AB blood type shows both A and B antigens). Incomplete: blend produced (red × white = pink). Students conflate these. |
| Dihybrid cross ratios | 9:3:3:1 is the standard ratio. Deviations (9:3:4, 12:3:1, 9:7, 15:1) signal gene interaction. Students expect 9:3:3:1 always. |
| Sex-linked traits | Carrier females (X^A X^a) are phenotypically normal. Affected males (X^a Y) show the trait. Students think carriers show the trait. |
| Multiple alleles (ABO) | Three alleles, but only two per individual. I^A and I^B are co-dominant. i is recessive to both. Genotype ≠ blood group in a simple lookup. |

**Quiet exceptional fact:** In sex-linked inheritance, affected fathers CANNOT pass the trait to sons (Y chromosome carries no allele). Affected mothers pass to ALL sons. JAMB constructs pedigrees specifically to test this directional logic.

---

#### 2. Cell Division — Sequence Precision
**Friction type:** Meiosis and mitosis look similar until the specific stage matters.

**Where it bites:**
- Crossing over occurs in **Prophase I** of meiosis only — not mitosis, not any other stage
- Homologous chromosome separation occurs in **Anaphase I** — sister chromatids separate in **Anaphase II**
- Mitosis produces 2 genetically identical diploid cells. Meiosis produces 4 genetically variable haploid cells.
- JAMB shows diagrams at specific stages — students must identify stage from chromosome arrangement, not from memory of the sequence

**Quiet exceptional fact:** A cell in Anaphase I of meiosis has the same number of chromosomes as a mitotic cell in Anaphase — but the genetic content and ploidy logic are completely different. JAMB uses this to create plausible wrong answers.

---

#### 3. Ecology — The Numerical Layer
**Quiet exceptional facts only.**

| Quiet exceptional fact | Why it bites |
|---|---|
| Pyramid of numbers can be inverted | A single oak tree supports thousands of insects. The pyramid inverts. Students think pyramids are always upright. |
| Pyramid of energy is NEVER inverted | Energy is always lost at each trophic level. Always upright. No exceptions. JAMB uses this as a definitive question. |
| Population growth: J vs S curve | J-curve = exponential (no limiting factors). S-curve = logistic (carrying capacity reached). JAMB asks what causes the curve to level off — the answer is always limiting factors, not population decline. |

### GOVERNMENT FRICTION ATLAS

Government's friction is structural, not notational. The quiet exceptional facts here are **definitional precision** — words that look synonymous but are legally and politically distinct.

#### Quiet Exceptional Facts

| Concept pair | The precise distinction |
|---|---|
| Sovereignty vs Independence | Sovereignty is the absolute authority of a state internally and externally. Independence is freedom from external control. A state can be independent but lack full sovereignty (e.g. under treaty obligations). |
| Confederation vs Federation | Confederation: component units are supreme, centre is weak and can be dissolved. Federation: central government is supreme in its sphere; states cannot secede. Students use them interchangeably. |
| Bicameral purpose | Upper house is not just "the other house." It exists to check hasty legislation, represent federating units, and provide institutional continuity (staggered elections). JAMB asks for the specific function. |
| De jure vs de facto recognition | De jure: full legal recognition under international law. De facto: practical recognition of control without legal endorsement. A government can be de facto without being de jure. |
| Rule of law (Dicey's three principles) | (1) Supremacy of law over arbitrary power. (2) Equality before the law. (3) Constitutional rights derived from ordinary law not guaranteed rights. Students know the phrase, not the three components. |
| Judicial independence mechanisms | Security of tenure + guaranteed remuneration + immunity from civil suit for judicial acts. JAMB asks specifically which mechanism protects which aspect of independence. |

**Friction type for Government:** The question always contains a qualifier — "MOST important," "PRIMARILY," "BEST describes." Students answer the general question, not the qualified question. English obfuscation layer is particularly active here.

### ECONOMICS FRICTION ATLAS

Economics is the dual-terrain subject — quantitative disguised as social science. The cannon visits frequently.

#### 1. Elasticity — The Coefficient Trap
**Friction type:** Four types of elasticity, similar formulas, different interpretations.

**Where it bites:**
- PED formula: `% change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price`. Students invert.
- PED is always negative (inverse relationship) — JAMB uses the absolute value. Students apply the sign and choose wrong option.
- Unitary elasticity: PED = 1. Total revenue unchanged. Students think revenue increases.
- Cross elasticity sign: positive = substitutes, negative = complements. Students forget the sign carries meaning.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** Income elasticity for inferior goods is negative. As income rises, quantity demanded falls. Students assume all goods have positive income elasticity.

---

#### 2. Market Structures
**Quiet exceptional facts only — standard definitions are taught everywhere.**

| Quiet exceptional fact | Why it bites |
|---|---|
| Oligopoly and kinked demand curve | Rivals match price cuts but ignore price rises — producing the kink. Implies price rigidity without explicit collusion. Students think oligopolies always collude formally. |
| Perfect competition: zero economic profit in long run | Normal profit is included in costs. Economic profit = zero. Students confuse accounting profit with economic profit. |
| Price discrimination conditions | Seller must have market power + ability to separate markets + prevent resale. All three must hold. Students name one condition and stop. |
| Monopsony | Single buyer, not single seller. JAMB tests whether students distinguish monopoly (seller power) from monopsony (buyer power). Labour markets frequently cited. |

---

#### 3. National Income Accounting
**Friction type:** Three methods, one answer — but the adjustments differ.

**Where it bites:**
- `GDP vs GNP`: GDP = output within borders. GNP = output by nationals regardless of location. `GNP = GDP + Net Factor Income from Abroad`.
- Transfer payments (pensions, unemployment benefits) are NOT included in GDP. They are not payments for current production. Students include them.
- Depreciation: `NDP = GDP - depreciation`. Students add depreciation.
- `GDP at market price vs factor cost`: `Factor cost = Market price - indirect taxes + subsidies`.

**Quiet exceptional fact:** Double counting is the conceptual trap in national income. The value-added method exists specifically to avoid it. JAMB tests whether students understand WHY intermediate goods are excluded — not just that they are.

## VI. THE ENGLISH OBFUSCATION LAYER — Applied to Every Subject

This section maps how English friction compounds subject-specific friction. When both layers are active simultaneously, a prepared student in the subject can still fail the question.

| Subject | Common obfuscation | How to decode |
|---|---|---|
| Chemistry/Electrolysis | Passive voice hides electrode identity — "the substance deposited at the electrode through which conventional current exits..." | Identify electrode by current direction, then rename: current exits = anode = oxidation |
| Chemistry/Equilibrium | "Least likely to shift" — qualifier buried after three conditions | Read the qualifier first. Circle it. Then evaluate each condition. |
| Physics/Projectiles | Conditional with non-standard angle — "if launched at an angle such that..." | Convert the conditional to a known case. Is it 45°? Is it horizontal? Name it. |
| Mathematics/Sets | "Neither A nor B" in word problem = belongs to complement of A∪B | Translate to set notation before calculating |
| Mathematics/Binomial | "Independent of x" = power of x is zero | Write the general term. Set the x-exponent to zero. Solve for r. Then find the term. |
| Government | "MOST effectively," "PRIMARILY," "BEST" — answer is always the most specific, most direct mechanism | Eliminate the general truths. The answer is always the precise one. |
| Economics | "CETERIS PARIBUS" — all other things equal — conditions the entire question | If another variable has been changed in the scenario, the ceteris paribus assumption is broken. Re-read. |
| Literature | Diction question — "the word X in line Y MOST NEARLY means..." | It is never the dictionary definition. It is always contextual. Read the two lines before and after. |

## VII. THE FACT-FILE PRINCIPLE

The textbook is 500 pages. The fact-file is under 100 pages. The difference is not omission — it is **consequence-ranked compression**.

A fact-file entry earns its place by satisfying at least one of these conditions:
1. It is a quiet exceptional fact — it appears in pro ceiling questions
2. It is a notation or symbol decode — it removes cognitive friction at the representation layer
3. It is a "why it exists" sentence — it provides the conceptual anchor that makes multiple surface facts memorable
4. It is a boundary condition — it defines where a formula, rule, or principle breaks down

**The "why it exists" sentence — the most powerful entry type:**

| Topic | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| Partial fractions | To decompose complex fractions into separately integrable pieces |
| Electrolysis calculation chain | To convert electrical quantity into deposited chemical mass |
| Modular arithmetic | To describe systems that repeat cyclically — clocks, calendars, cryptography |
| Binomial theorem | To expand powers of binomials without repeated multiplication |
| Equilibrium constant | To quantify exactly where a reversible reaction settles |
| Mole concept | To count atoms and molecules at a scale humans can measure |
| Elasticity | To measure the sensitivity of economic quantity to price change |
| Separation of powers | To prevent any one institution from accumulating total authority |

The fact-file is not a summary. It is a **precision instrument** for removing the exact frictions that produce wrong answers in prepared students.

## IX. AGENT OPERATING PROTOCOL

Any agent working within the GrantEd system operates under these principles — not as external commands but as internalized operational standards:

**Before generating any question:**
- Identify the subject's friction atlas position for this topic
- Assign triage tag before writing options
- Check whether English obfuscation layer is appropriate for the level
- Confirm the distractor logic — wrong options must represent real failure modes, not random errors

**Before writing any fact-file entry:**
- Does this entry decode a notation problem, provide a "why it exists" sentence, or flag a quiet exceptional fact?
- If none of the above — does it appear in pro ceiling questions?
- If still none — it does not earn a place in the fact-file

**Before building any quiz or cluster:**
- Sprint questions should outnumber Return questions 2:1
- Pro ceiling questions: maximum 5 per 40-question set — proportionally fewer in clusters
- Every set should contain at least one English obfuscation layer question regardless of subject
